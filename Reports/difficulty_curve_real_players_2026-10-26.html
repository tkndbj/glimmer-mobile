Ladder difficulty curve: real players since 1 October

Gemfire, Thornwatch ladder. Cohort: every account created 1–26 Oct 2026 (the Meta campaign window), located by the IP address of its first server call, with Cyprus, review/test bots and the test suite removed. 3,237 real players out of 3,381 new accounts. Sources: Firestore cloud saves, Cloud Functions request logs (IP), DB-IP Lite (offline IP → country), and GA4. Read-only audit: nothing in the game, saves or config was changed.

90%never finish chapter 1 (333 of 3,237 reach level 11)
L4 & L6the two walls: 29% stop at thornhollow, 38% at bramblerun
15% / 4%still playing after day 1 / after day 7 (rolling retention)
4.5%of continue offers end in a watched video (108 of 2,416); 2.0% in a gem continue
The headline. For the players the campaigns bought, the ladder's difficulty problem is entirely in chapter 1. Level 4 stops 29% of the players who reach it and level 6 stops 38%. Each is lost at least once by about 29% of its players, and about 42% of those losers never touch the level again. After chapter 1 the curve is gentle: most levels stop fewer than 10% of their players. The bigger lever may be markets rather than levels. Brazil (74% of the cohort) loses 10% of its runs; Mexico loses 28% and the Gulf 35%, so the same chapter 1 is far harder for them. Overall day-7 retention is 4% (0–6% by region).

3. Hearts

The hearts rule: a loss costs 1 heart, except in the first 10 levels of a mode and on any level already cleared. Hearts cap at 5 and refill one every 8 hours. Since 1.1.2 (22 Oct) the free window is 10 levels, up from 3, so all of chapter 1 is heart-free. Players on older builds paid hearts on L4–L10.

  • So hearts are not what stops people. Players quit at L4 and L6 after a loss even though, today, that loss costs nothing.

7. What this means

  1. Watch L4 and L6 on 1.1.3 for a week. If the L4 drop falls from 29% towards the 12–14% baseline, the main wall is gone.
  2. L8 blackmarch and L10 lastlight are next. 32% and 40% of their players lose at least once, and about 25–27% of their winners lost first. They still keep most players (stop 22% and 18%) because their losers come back: only 15–17% never retry, against 41–43% on L4 and L6.
  3. L4's real cost is losers walking away. 29% of its players lose, but only 12% of its winners lost first. Most of the players it beats don't come back.
  4. Chapter 1 is much harder in Mexico and the Gulf than in Brazil. 28% and 35% of their runs are lost, against 10% in Brazil. Only 8% of Mexico and 2% of the Gulf reach chapter 2, against 12% of Brazil. The likely cause is the audience the campaigns reached, not the levels (the content is identical). If Meta budget is moving between markets, cost per player who reaches L11 is the number to compare, not cost per install.
  5. L1 is an onboarding leak. 14% leave before clearing a level nobody loses. Look at the tutorial and first session.
  6. Retention is the bigger story. Day-1 15%, day-7 4%. Even players who clear chapter 1 mostly stop within a week. The difficulty fixes help, but they won't close that gap alone.
  7. Hearts are not the bottleneck: only about 3% of players ever hit an empty heart bar.

Gaps found in the measurement (read-only findings, nothing changed)

  • Only level_id and first_clear are registered GA4 custom dimensions, and first_clear holds no usable values (the game sends it as a number). Register reason, hearts_left, stars, attempt, chapter_id, video, choice, won, placement. They collect only from the day they're added.
  • The BigQuery export has only been linked since 24 Oct 23:17.
  • level_started doesn't fire on an in-place retry, so starts undercount attempts.
  • Players can only be located through the seeded a new wallet log line joined to the request log. Cloud Logging keeps 30 days, so the oldest installs fall out of reach from about 26 Oct onward. Nothing in the game records a player's country.
  • Your test devices on Starlink in Cyprus, and Apple/Google review bots, create new accounts in production. They're excluded here but sit in every other server-side figure.

Method

  • Who: each account's first getWallet call logs seeded a new wallet {uid}. Its Cloud Run request log, joined by trace id, carries the caller's IP. The IP is mapped to a country and network with the DB-IP Lite databases (CC BY 4.0, offline), so no IP left this machine. This is the IP at install time; a player on a VPN is placed where the VPN exits.
  • Saves: only cleared levels are stored, never failed attempts. So the saves answer "where do players stop", and GA answers "where do players lose". Last activity = the later of the last cloud sync and the last level played.
  • GA4: Data API, read-only. Country from GA's own IP geolocation. Per-level figures and per-level funnels cover 24–26 Oct and are broken down by country, with Cyprus removed.
